## Executive findings

None of the three rewrite targets clears the 10x bar.

- `vehicle-trim-levels-explained`: the production page and package both answer the basic definition, but neither is a trustworthy, complete answer to the query cluster. The production page has two H1s and no FAQ schema. The package has malformed nested headings, unsupported/generalized trim advice, and FAQ schema contaminated with Highlander questions.
- `highlander-vs-grand-highlander`: the package is closer to the query intent than the production page, but it has duplicate IDs, duplicate/nested H2s, thin answers, imprecise sourcing, and model-year ambiguity. The production page has two H1s and makes several unverified inventory, passenger-fit, price, and fuel-economy claims.
- `heavy-duty-vs-light-duty-trucks`: the production blog is materially broken. Its “What Is a Heavy-Duty Truck?” section contains spring tire and alignment copy rather than a heavy-duty-truck definition. Its capability ranges are unsourced approximations. A dedicated answer page is justified because the query needs a durable, comparison-first resource, not a repair to a dated four-minute blog post.
- New-page SERP limitation: Google returned no extractable organic results, DuckDuckGo returned a block page, and Bing returned irrelevant token/dictionary results for all three exact queries. Per the directive, the new-page analyses therefore use the flagged-query table and the site’s existing content as the gap base. No pages are represented below as actual top-three competitors.
- Decision 6: augment `/aboutus.aspx`; do not create a competing Spirit Lake route unless DealerOn rejects the existing-route edit. The production HTML has a discrete `#content-main.About-Us-page` region under `#content-description`, the same editable content pattern used by the package lane. GSC already associates the 557-impression query with `/aboutus.aspx`, so augmentation preserves that signal and avoids cannibalization.

## Rewrite audit 1: trim-levels-explained

Target URL: `/vehicle-trim-levels-explained`

Flagged demand: about 1,818 impressions over six months across the cluster.

### What the production page does now

It defines a trim as an equipment package and gives Toyota and Chevrolet examples. It includes a quick-reference ladder, use-case prompts, local inventory links, and an early CTA. Those are useful raw components.

It still fails the bar:

- Two H1s are rendered: “Vehicle Trim Levels Explained | Carroll, IA” and “Car Trim Levels Explained.”
- The first answer is not in the first sentence of the page. The introductory paragraph delays the definition.
- There is no direct answer to the 954-impression phrase, “some car manufacturers use this to describe certain trims of a car.” The page should explain that the intended term is usually **grade** or **series**, depending on the manufacturer/context, rather than pretending the phrase is simply another definition query. This needs primary-source confirmation before drafting.
- The synthetic-upholstery cluster is absent. No SofTex answer appears in the production body.
- The truck-specific wording is not answered as its own question.
- No FAQPage schema is present.
- The Toyota/Chevrolet ladder overgeneralizes changing lineups. Examples such as XLE/XSE as a generic Toyota “comfort mid” ladder and 1LT as a Chevrolet work-entry counterpart are model-dependent, not universal.
- “TRD for trails and towing on rough roads” conflates appearance/performance packages, off-road equipment, and towing. Exact capability depends on model, configuration, and tow equipment.
- “XLE, Limited, LT, or High Country add the safety and comfort features that matter most” is both subjective and unsourced.
- “Stock changes daily” is an operational cadence claim without evidence and should be removed or verified.
- The CTA says “One change per page,” which is stray production/process language and not shopper-facing copy.
- The content uses multiple CTAs and choices even though the house method calls for one obvious next step.

### What the existing package improves

The package moves the core definition and synthetic-upholstery response into a quick-answer box, adds FAQPage JSON-LD, preserves the self-canonical, includes source links, and is more concise than the production page.

### What fails in the package

- The main body is only about 789 words including chrome and gives one-sentence treatment to major questions. It is an answer stub, not a 10x explanation.
- The markup contains an H2 inside another H2 for “Vehicle trim FAQs,” producing a malformed heading tree.
- FAQ schema is contaminated with four unrelated Highlander questions. It must be regenerated only from visible trim-level questions.
- The visible page does not contain truck-specific H2s even though truck questions appear only in schema. Schema must not be used to hide coverage that shoppers cannot read.
- The quick answer names RAV4 SofTex without identifying exact model year and trim(s), while the linked source is a mechanical-performance page rather than an upholstery/interior-feature source.
- “Toyota examples run SR, SR5, XLE, XSE, TRD, Limited, Platinum, and Capstone” combines names from different vehicle lines as if they form one consistent ladder.
- The “best for” table makes unsourced recommendations. “TRD Sport” is grouped with off-road choices even though Sport and Off-Road are not interchangeable.

- The SEL answer introduces Hyundai Santa Cruz on a Toyota/Chevrolet dealership page without explaining that trim acronyms are manufacturer-specific. It does not answer what SEL means, and the wording could date quickly.
- The source block is too coarse. It does not map each changing lineup/material claim to an exact source.
- The 59-character metadata title is within the later hygiene target; the content and markup, not title length, are the blockers.
- The page class is copied from a Highlander comparison (`2024-Toyota-Highlander-vs-2024-Toyota-Grand-Highlander--page`), evidence of template leakage.

## Rewrite audit 3: heavy-duty-vs-light-duty-trucks

### What fails in the production blog

- The page does not answer either flagged query in sentence one or in a dedicated question heading.
- The section headed “What Is a Heavy-Duty Truck?” contains unrelated spring tire, tread-depth, tire-pressure, pothole, and alignment copy. The core definition is missing.
- The post gives unsourced approximate ranges: under “~8,500 pounds” GVWR, towing “up to around 12,000 pounds,” payload “1,500–3,000 pounds,” heavy-duty towing “20,000+,” and payload “6,000+.” These are neither configuration-specific nor tied to Ford/GM/Ram tow guides.
- It treats “light duty” as a single spec class and “heavy duty” as another without explaining manufacturer naming, federal weight classes, half-ton/three-quarter-ton/one-ton shorthand, or the importance of each truck’s door-jamb payload label.

- “The investment in a heavy-duty truck pays off” is promotional and unsupported.
- “Peace of mind,” “perfect match,” “great selection,” and “make an informed decision” conflict with the Voice DNA anti-slop standard.
- The post has no FAQPage schema and no direct primary-source citations.
- The CTA link text “schedule a day to test drive” is awkward.

### Decision for Spencer: page versus blog

**Build the dedicated page package.** Keep the blog as a supporting article and link it to the page; do not make the broken blog the canonical answer asset.

Rationale:

- The query is evergreen and comparison/decision intent, which fits an owned answer page better than a dated post.
- A page can use question-led sections, a sourced comparison table, FAQ schema, exact inventory/contact links, and ongoing spec refreshes without preserving blog date/read-time framing.
- The existing blog’s missing heavy-duty definition and unrelated tire copy make augmentation a repair project before it can even compete.
- The new page can own the stable conceptual answer; the blog can later be corrected and narrowed to a supporting practical checklist.
- Do not redirect or canonicalize the blog during Phase 1. Later implementation should decide whether to retain both with distinct intent or consolidate only after link/index evidence is reviewed.
